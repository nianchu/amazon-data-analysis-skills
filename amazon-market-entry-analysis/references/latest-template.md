# Latest reusable report template

Use the V10 decision-first workbook as the visual and narrative baseline. Recalculate all content for the current product and marketplace; never reuse HDMI-specific conclusions as universal facts.

## Reader-facing order

1. Executive decision and action plan
2. Metric dictionary and scope
3. Market capacity
4. Sales trend
5. Demand trend: Amazon keywords and separate Google Trends
6. Lifecycle
7. Seasonality and marketing events
8. Mainstream product forms, price bands, and listing-age layers
9. New-product entry opportunity
10. Link and brand competition
11. Keyword conversion and CPC ranking
12. Feature-parameter decision and controlled detail
13. Three competitor operating breakdowns
14. Consumer scenarios
15. Review themes, shares, pain points, and improvements
16. Profit
17. Compliance and patent gate
18. Calculation bases, formal pools, integrated sources, historical sources, and review sources

All product-pool and source-data sheets must remain last.

## Executive conclusion

Lead with one of: recommend entry, conditional entry, defer entry, or insufficient evidence.

Show:

- market position;
- target segment and one primary SKU;
- recommended specifications and ports;
- price and profit;
- demand and trend evidence;
- competition and new-product risk;
- review-backed product improvements;
- main use scenarios;
- 30/60/90-day operating plan;
- data limitations and compliance gates.

Do not leave stale “pending” statements after a module has been completed.

## Core calculations

- Market capacity = sum of child revenue in the qualified formal pool for the stated period. Label it a reported lower bound when sales fields are missing.
- Market units = sum of child sales in the same grain and period.
- Link/brand CRn = top-n qualified revenue divided by qualified reported revenue.
- Lifecycle = mutually exclusive listing-age cohorts with product, unit, and revenue shares.
- Price bands = mutually exclusive observed-price bands with parameter, function, listing-age, unit, and revenue views.
- Feature analysis = parameter coverage + stratified comparisons + feature combinations. Never equate association with causation.
- Keyword demand = source search volume, purchase volume, conversion evidence, CPC, competition, and source supply-demand ratio. A sum across overlapping keywords is an index, not unique shoppers.
- Review theme coverage = unique reviews tagged with a theme divided by deduplicated valid reviews. A review may have multiple themes.
- Review positive share = positive reviews within the theme divided by reviews tagged with the theme.
- Volumetric first-leg cost = `(length × width × height ÷ volumetric divisor) × volumetric-weight rate`.
- Actual-weight first-leg cost = `actual packaged weight × actual-weight rate`.
- Final first-leg cost = `MAX(volumetric first-leg cost, actual-weight first-leg cost)`; retain both calculations and the selected method.
- Profit = selling price minus purchase, first-leg, referral, FBA, withdrawal, return, storage, advertising, and other costs.
- Opportunity score = profit 30 + competition accessibility 30 + new-product sell-through 20 + market capacity/trend 20. A compliance or margin blocker overrides the score.

## Evidence standards learned from the latest project

- Define the product positively before excluding adjacent products.
- Run historical product pools through the same qualification rules.
- Preserve Listing-unreported parameters; do not convert them to “unsupported.”
- Show parameter distributions, not only the mode, so important minority specifications remain visible.
- Keep Google Trends separate from Amazon search volume.
- Compare historical periods only when keyword/product scope is like-for-like; otherwise label growth directional.
- For reviews, show review-weighted, ASIN-balanced, sales-weighted, and recent-period results when possible; filter irrelevant variations and manually QA rule-based themes.
- Put inputs below a simple cost/profit view and ask the user to confirm commercially material values before calculating.
- Treat patent/compliance content as a gate and checklist until actual evidence or professional clearance exists.
- Keep the final conclusion actionable: one main SKU, a controlled price test, evidence-based scenes, numerical operating gates, and explicit stop conditions.
